AWS S3 Storage Cost Calculator

This line-item estimate adds storage, requests and retrieval. The defaults are an editable US East S3 Standard example at $0.023 per billable GB-month, with 1 TiB represented as 1,024 GB. One million PUTs and ten million GETs bring the subtotal to $32.55 before other charges. For the full rate structure and a worked 1 TiB example, read the AWS S3 pricing guide.

Billing realism checks

Three class-specific rules move an S3 bill more than the headline rate does: volume tiers, a minimum billable object size, and a minimum storage duration. The defaults trigger none of them: 1,024 GB sits inside the first tier, no object minimum is set, and 30-day retention meets a 30-day minimum. Enter a non-zero object count and a non-zero minimum billable size and storage is recomputed as objects × max(average size, minimum size), which replaces the billable-GB field above rather than adjusting it.

Standard-IA, One Zone-IA and Glacier Instant Retrieval bill every object at a 128 KiB minimum, so a million 10 KiB objects are charged as 122 GB. Minimum storage durations are 30 days (Standard-IA, One Zone-IA), 90 days (Glacier Instant Retrieval, Glacier Flexible Retrieval) and 180 days (Glacier Deep Archive).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does 1 TiB of S3 Standard storage cost per month?

At the modeled example US East list price of $0.023 per billable GB-month, 1,024 billable GB for 1 TiB costs $23.55 a month before request and retrieval fees. This calculator adds PUT and GET fees on top.

Do S3 PUT and GET requests add much to the bill?

They can. At the modeled example US East Standard rates, one million PUT requests cost $5.00 at $0.005 per thousand. Ten million GET requests cost $4.00 at $0.0004 per thousand. Rates vary by storage class and Region.

What costs are not included in this S3 estimate?

The calculator adds storage, entered PUT/GET request fees and entered retrieval, and it applies volume tiers, a per-object minimum size and a minimum storage duration when you supply the object count and average retention. It does not model data transfer out, lifecycle transitions, versioning overhead, per-object age, or request types other than PUT and GET. Check the current AWS S3 price for your Region and class.

How is the monthly S3 cost calculated?

Storage is split across the three volume tiers, then multiplied by the minimum-duration factor when your average retention is shorter than the class minimum. Request fees divide each count by 1,000 before applying its rate, and retrieval adds gigabytes times its rate. The defaults follow S3 Standard US East list prices with no per-object minimum and a 30-day retention, so the duration factor is 1.00× and the total is unchanged from a flat-rate estimate.
